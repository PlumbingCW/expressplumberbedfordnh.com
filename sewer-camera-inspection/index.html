Sewer Camera Inspection in Bedford, NH

.

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible cable into your drain line and records what it finds, from root intrusion to cracked pipe to a low spot holding water. It turns a repeat clog or a pre-purchase worry into something you can see.

In Bedford, NH most inspections are scheduled ahead of a home purchase or after a line has clogged more than once. If sewage is already backing up, call the 24/7 line first, because clearing the line comes before looking at it.

Call (603) 815-9179.

What the camera shows

.

The camera head carries lights and a transmitter, so we can see inside the pipe and locate any problem from the surface. A monitor shows the picture live, and we record the run so you have a copy.

  • Roots entering at joints or cracks.
  • Cracked, broken, offset or collapsed sections.
  • Bellies, which are low spots that hold water and waste.
  • Scale, grease and debris that narrow the pipe.
  • The pipe material and any change in material along the run.
  • Connections from other lines and the condition of the cleanout.

Knowing the pipe material matters in Bedford, where homes from different decades use cast iron, clay, Orangeburg or PVC, and each fails in a different way.

When an inspection is worth booking

.

Most people book one for a specific reason.

Situation.Why a camera helps.
Buying a home.Reveals problems a general home inspection does not reach, before you commit.
Repeat clogs.Shows whether the pipe is damaged or just coated.
Slow drains after cleaning.Confirms the line is open and finds anything left behind.
Before a repair or lining.Maps the exact location and length of damage.
Sinkhole or wet patch in the yard.Traces whether the sewer is the source.
Old house with unknown pipe.Identifies material and condition for planning.

If a clog is active, we clear it first because the camera cannot see through standing waste. The camera then runs on the cleaned line.

Buying or selling a home in Bedford

.

A buyer who skips the sewer camera takes on the line along with the house. Repair or replacement of a failed sewer can run into several thousand dollars, and it comes up after closing when the first backup occurs.

A seller can also use a clean inspection as a point of confidence. If the camera shows a problem, you learn it with time to negotiate, plan the repair or adjust the price. Ask your real estate agent how the timing fits your inspection period. Owners of older houses can also read our Historic Town Center page for questions worth asking before a repair.

What you get after the visit

.

You receive the recording and a plain-language summary of what we saw. We note the distance from the access point to each finding, the pipe type and whether the line looks sound, needs cleaning or needs repair.

If repair is needed, we outline the options and the likely range. See sewer line repair for how spot repair, lining and replacement compare, or hydro jetting if scale or grease is the issue.

What affects the cost of a camera inspection

.

Insurers sometimes ask for an inspection when a sewer claim is opened, and some policies reimburse it. Ask yours before the visit.

Common questions

.
How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most take under an hour from access to summary. Longer lines, difficult access or cleaning first adds time.

Will the camera damage my pipes?

No. The head is flexible and sized for the line. We do not push through a line we believe is collapsed.

Do I get a copy of the video?

Yes. You receive the recording and a written summary of findings and their location.

Can you inspect a line that is backed up?

We need to clear it first. Call the 24/7 line for an active backup and we will inspect after it is open.

Is an inspection worth it when buying a house?

In most cases yes, because sewer failures are expensive and hidden. It is a small cost compared with an unexpected replacement.

Do you inspect lines in other towns?

Yes. We serve Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.
