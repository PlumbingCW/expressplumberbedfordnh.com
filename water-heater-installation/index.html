Water Heater Installation in Bedford, NH

.

A planned water heater installation lets you choose the unit, the size and the day, instead of taking whatever is in stock after a tank fails. Most installs are scheduled within a few days, and our dispatch line is staffed 24/7 to book one.

Water heater installation is the setting, connecting and testing of a new unit, including venting, gas or electric hookup, relief valve and drain. In Bedford, NH this covers conventional tanks, hybrid heat pump models and new gas or electric lines for added capacity.

Choosing the right type of heater

.

The right unit depends on fuel, space, household size and budget. These are the main categories.

Type.Best for.Watch for.
Electric tank.Homes with no gas and a simple install.Slower recovery, higher running cost.
Gas tank.Homes with gas service and a working flue.Venting and combustion air requirements.
Heat pump (hybrid).Homeowners who want lower electricity use.Needs clearance, a drain and a space that stays above freezing.
Tankless.Small spaces and large households.Higher upfront cost and gas or electrical upgrades.

A longer comparison of tankless units is on our tankless water heater page, and if you are replacing a failed unit, see water heater replacement.

Sizing for a Bedford household

.

The first-hour rating on a tank tells you how much hot water it delivers in a busy hour, which matters more than the gallon size. A household with two showers running at once while a dishwasher cycles needs more than a couple that bathes at different times.

Many Bedford homes have large primary bathrooms and soaking tubs, and one 40-gallon tank cannot keep up. We ask about your busiest morning and size the heater to match.

What a proper installation includes

.

A careful job is more than hooking up pipes. The steps matter because they decide how long the heater lasts and how safe it is.

  1. Confirm fuel type, venting, gas line size or circuit capacity.
  2. Set the heater on a drain pan with a drain line, when the location needs one.
  3. Install new shutoff valves, flexible connectors and a dielectric union or equivalent where metals differ.
  4. Fit a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe to a safe termination.
  5. Add or replace the expansion tank where the system needs one.
  6. Connect the flue or power, fill the tank, purge air, test for leaks and set the temperature.

Permit and inspection requirements vary by town and fuel type, so ask the Bedford town office whether your install needs one. If it does, we tell you what to expect.

Upgrading to a heat pump or going tankless

.

Heat pump heaters use electricity to move heat from the surrounding air into the tank. They suit basements that are cool and dry, and they bring a small amount of cooling and dehumidifying to the room.

Tankless units heat water on demand and take much less space, but they draw large amounts of gas or power while running. A gas tankless often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and an electric one may need a heavy circuit that the panel cannot support.

Water quality and your heater

.

If you are on Manchester Water Works, the 2018 report lists very soft water at 19.35 mg/L total hardness, which is easy on tanks. Private well water is different, and NHDES recommends testing every three to five years.

Iron, sediment and hardness shorten the life of a heater. If a well test shows any of them, a filter or softener ahead of the heater can protect the new unit.

Common questions

.
How long does a water heater installation take?

A straight swap often takes a few hours. A change in fuel, venting or location takes longer and may need a second visit.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on the town and the type of work. Ask the Bedford town office, and we will tell you what we find that could affect it.

What size heater should I choose?

Look at the first-hour rating and your busiest hour. A short conversation about bathrooms and appliances settles it.

Can you move my water heater?

Yes. Relocating a unit means new supply, vent and drain lines, and we quote that as part of the job.

Do you remove the old heater?

Yes. Haul-away is included in the job.

Do you install in nearby towns?

Yes. We serve Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. For private wells, the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services recommends testing every three to five years.
