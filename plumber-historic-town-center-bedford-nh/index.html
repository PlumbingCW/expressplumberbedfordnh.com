Where the Historic Town Center is

.

Bedford Town Hall stands at 70 Bedford Center Road, at the junction of Bedford Center Road and Meetinghouse Road. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and it sits among the town's civic buildings.

Local real estate guides place the center west of the Meetinghouse Road and Route 101 intersection. We do not draw an exact boundary for this page, because the town has not published one that we could verify. If your street is near the center, call and the dispatcher will confirm it is within our work area.

What we know and what we do not

.

We know the center is a civic and historic part of Bedford. We do not know the age, pipe materials or plumbing history of any particular house here, and we will not guess from the look of a building.

Age alone does not tell you what is inside a wall. A house can have had parts of its plumbing replaced several times, so the only reliable answer comes from opening a panel or putting a camera in the line.

Older homes: ask before you assume

.

If you own or are buying an older house near the center, a few questions are worth answering before any repair is priced.

  • When was the water heater last replaced, and is the date on the tank label?
  • Has the kitchen, bath or basement been renovated, and who did the plumbing work?
  • Where is the main water shutoff, and does it turn freely?
  • Do the drains clear slowly in more than one fixture?

A sewer camera inspection answers the drain question with a picture instead of an opinion. For visible supply pipes, we look at what is actually there and explain it in plain terms.

Utilities and permits: ask the town

.

Water and sewer service differ from one Bedford street to the next, and we do not assume which one applies to your house. Your water bill shows whether you are on public water, and the town can tell you about sewer or septic for your address.

Permit rules and code editions are set locally. Ask the Bedford building department what applies before a larger job, and we will work to what the town requires.

Common questions

.
Is the Historic Town Center inside your work area?

Yes, our office is in Bedford and we take calls in the town. Give the dispatcher your street and cross street so the plumber knows exactly where to go.

Do you know what pipes are in my older house?

Not until we see them. House age does not tell us the pipe material, so we inspect first and then explain what we find and what it would cost to fix.

Who do I ask about sewer or water service?

Your water bill shows your water provider, and the Bedford town offices can tell you whether your address is on sewer or a septic system.

Can I get a same-day appointment?

Same-day appointments are offered when a plumber is available. Call early in the day and describe the problem so the dispatcher can plan the route.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project.
