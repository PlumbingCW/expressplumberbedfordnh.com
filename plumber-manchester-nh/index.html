Emergency plumbing in Manchester: what we respond to

.

The calls that come in from Manchester are a mix of the same few emergencies, and each has a first step you can take before we arrive.

  • A burst or frozen pipe in an apartment, house or storefront.
  • Sewage or water rising from a floor drain, tub or toilet.
  • A leaking or failed water heater tank, day or night.
  • A clogged main line in a multi-unit building affecting several tenants.
  • No water, or a broken service line in the yard.

Tell the dispatcher your street and which ZIP code you are in, so the call goes to the nearest free plumber. Same-day appointments are offered when a plumber is available, and nights and weekends are covered like any other hour.

Mills, apartments and older homes

.

The Amoskeag Millyard along the Merrimack River is the best-known part of Manchester's building stock, and the city around it is full of multi-unit houses, triple-deckers and older single-family homes. Buildings of different ages share the same sewer system, and the pipe inside each one reflects the era it was built.

That variety shapes the work. A multi-unit building with a shared main line sends a clog to the lowest unit first, which is why sewer line cleaning is often the right first call. When the same line keeps clogging, a camera inspection shows whether the pipe is worn or just coated.

Manchester Water Works and what it means for your pipes

.

Most of the city is supplied by Manchester Water Works, which draws from Lake Massabesic and has served the city and portions of six surrounding communities since 1874. Its report for 2018 lists total hardness at 19.35 mg/L and pH 7.8, and a lead 90th percentile under 1 ppb.

That is soft water, so mineral scale builds slowly in fixtures and tanks here compared with many well-water towns. Old service lines and interior pipes are still the issue to watch, and you can ask the utility what material your service line is made of.

Before we arrive in Manchester: what to do right now

.

Plumbing emergencies feel faster than they are. These five steps buy you time and reduce damage while a plumber is on the way.

Find the main shutoff.

.

It is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the basement. Close it clockwise if water is flowing.

Stop using drains.

.

If sewage is rising, do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher.

Protect belongings.

.

Lift boxes, electronics and rugs off wet floors.

Switch off power if safe.

.

Only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.

Take photos.

.

Capture the damage before cleanup for your insurer or landlord.
