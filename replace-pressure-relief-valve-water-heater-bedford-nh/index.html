Replacing a Pressure Relief Valve on a Hot Water Heater in Bedford, NH

.

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the last safety device on a water heater. If the tank gets too hot or too pressurized, the valve opens and lets water out so the tank cannot rupture.

A valve that drips is telling you something. This guide explains what, and how to replace one correctly.

What the valve does

.

A T&P valve sits on the side or top of the tank with a discharge pipe running down toward a safe place. It opens at a set pressure or temperature, which is printed on the valve and the tank label.

It is a safety device, not a part to plug, cap or ignore. Never block the discharge pipe, and never replace a valve with one of a different rating.

Why a relief valve drips

.

Dripping has a few distinct causes, and the fix depends on which one you have.

Cause.Sign.Fix.
Worn or scaled valve seat.Slow drip, no pressure problem.Replace the valve.
High water pressure.Drips when the heater is not running.Test pressure, add or replace a pressure reducing valve.
Failed expansion tank.Drips as the water heats.Test and replace the expansion tank.
Overheated tank.Steam or very hot water at the valve.Shut off and call a professional.
Thermostat failure.Water too hot at the tap.Replace the thermostat.

A valve that drips only when the heater fires up usually means thermal expansion has nowhere to go, and a new valve will not solve that.

Testing the valve

.

Place a bucket under the discharge pipe, then lift the test lever briefly. Water should flow out and stop when you release it.

If nothing comes out, the valve is stuck, and if it keeps dripping after you release it, debris is holding it open. Either way, plan on replacement. Do not test a valve you suspect is failing while the tank is overheated.

Replacing the valve

.

Turn off power or gas to the heater and close the cold-water supply. Open a hot tap and drain enough water to bring the level below the valve port.

  1. Disconnect the discharge pipe from the valve.
  2. Unscrew the old valve with a pipe wrench.
  3. Wrap the new valve's threads with pipe tape or compound.
  4. Thread it in by hand and tighten with the wrench.
  5. Reconnect the discharge pipe, which must slope down and end a few inches above the floor or in a drain.
  6. Refill the tank, open a hot tap until water flows steadily and restore power or gas.

Use a valve with the same temperature and pressure rating as the old one, and make sure its probe reaches into the tank.

When to call a plumber

.

Replacing a valve is within reach of many homeowners, but several situations call for help.

  • The valve is releasing steam or scalding water.
  • The valve is corroded and the threads do not budge.
  • The discharge pipe is missing, capped or runs uphill.
  • The valve drips again right after you replaced it.
  • The tank itself is leaking, which means water heater replacement.

A recurring drip often points to high house pressure, which a plumber can measure with a gauge. A pressure reducing valve or a new expansion tank solves it.

Thermal expansion and your home's water pressure

.

When a water heater warms, the water inside expands. In an open system, that extra volume pushes back into the supply line. Many homes have a backflow device or a pressure reducing valve that closes that path, so the water has nowhere to go and the pressure spikes.

An expansion tank absorbs the extra volume. It has an air cushion that must be charged to match your house pressure, and the cushion fails over time. A tank that has lost its air is a common reason for a dripping relief valve.

Ask a plumber to read your house pressure with a gauge on a hose bib. Compare the reading with the pressure range your fixtures and heater are rated for, and a regulator brings a high reading back into range.

Common questions

.
Is a dripping relief valve dangerous?

It is a warning, not an emergency by itself. It becomes urgent if the water is scalding or steaming, in which case shut off power or gas and call a plumber.

Can I cap the discharge pipe to stop the drip?

No. Capping the pipe defeats the safety device and can lead to tank failure. Fix the cause instead.

How often should I test the valve?

Many manufacturers suggest testing once a year. Follow the instructions on your tank.

Why does my valve drip only in the morning?

Heating after a long idle period raises the pressure, and with no room for expansion, the valve opens. An expansion tank usually solves it.

How do I check a plumber's license in New Hampshire?

Plumbers are not in the online lookup, so contact the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification directly.

.
