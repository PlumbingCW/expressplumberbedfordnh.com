Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Bedford, NH

.

Sewage in a basement or a main line that will not stay clear is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is staffed at any hour. While you wait, stop using water, keep people and pets out of the wet area and photograph the damage.

Sewer line repair is the fixing or replacement of a damaged section of the pipe that carries waste from your house to the street main or septic tank. In Bedford, NH the repair method is chosen after a camera shows exactly where and how the pipe has failed.

When a sewer line failure is an emergency

.

A failed sewer line puts raw waste where people live, and every flush or load of laundry makes it worse.

  • Sewage rises from a floor drain, tub or toilet.
  • Every drain in the house is slow or gurgling at once.
  • A sewage smell in the basement or yard that does not go away.
  • A sinkhole or soft patch of lawn over the sewer path.

Call at any hour and say sewage is involved, because the call is placed ahead of routine work. Same-day assessment is available when a plumber is free.

  1. Stop using every drain, toilet and appliance that discharges water.
  2. Keep children and pets away from the wet area and wear gloves if you must enter.
  3. Move belongings off the floor and open windows for air.
  4. Do not use chemical drain openers in a sewage backup.

What causes sewer lines to fail

.

Most Bedford houses were built after 1950, when the town began to grow from about 2,000 residents toward more than 23,000. Many of those homes have a building sewer of cast iron, clay or Orangeburg fiber pipe, all of which wear out in different ways.

  • Cast iron rusts from the inside and flakes into the flow.
  • Clay pipe joints open and let roots in.
  • Orangeburg fiber pipe softens and can flatten or collapse.
  • Ground shifts and freeze-thaw cycles crack rigid pipe or pull joints apart.
  • A low spot, or belly, holds water and waste so that clogs keep forming.

A homeowner may notice only the symptom, which is why a camera inspection is the first step. It turns a guess into a picture of the pipe.

Repair options after the camera

.

Once we see the problem, there are usually two or three ways forward. The right one depends on depth, length, what sits above the pipe and what you plan to do with the property.

Option.How it works.Good fit.
Spot repair.Dig and replace one short damaged section.A single break at a known, reachable spot.
Full replacement.Dig and install new pipe the full length.Pipe that is failing all along or sags badly.
Pipe lining.Cure a new liner inside the old pipe without a trench.Long runs under driveways, patios or landscaping.
Pipe bursting.Pull new pipe through as the old pipe is broken.Replacement where digging is hard.

We explain which options your pipe qualifies for, what each involves and what it costs, so you choose with a number in front of you.

Septic or town sewer

.

Not every Bedford property is on town sewer. A house on a septic system has a different set of failure points, including the tank, baffles, distribution box and leach field, and a full or failing tank looks just like a clogged main.

Tell the dispatcher which you have. If you are on town sewer and the problem is beyond your property line, the public main belongs to the town, and the town should be called as soon as you confirm the blockage is outside your cleanout.

What happens on the day of a repair

.

We start with the camera so we know the exact distance and depth of the damage. Utilities are marked before anyone digs, and New Hampshire expects 811 to be called before excavation.

Once the trench is open, we cut out the failed section, bed new pipe at the right slope, test the line and restore the trench. On trenchless jobs we clean the pipe, install the liner and run the camera again to show the finished result.

Permit rules differ by town and by repair type, so ask the Bedford town office whether your job needs one. We will tell you what we find and what the work involves.

Common questions

.
Do you handle sewer line emergencies at night or on weekends in Bedford?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7. A sewage backup is placed ahead of routine calls, and same-day assessment is offered when a plumber is free.

How do I know whether the problem is the sewer line?

If several fixtures back up at once, or the lowest drain overflows when you run water upstairs, the main line is the likely cause. A camera inspection confirms it.

Does a repair always mean digging up the yard?

No. Many lines can be lined or burst without a full trench, though pipes with severe collapse or deep bellies may need excavation.

Who is responsible for the part under the street?

The public main belongs to the town or sewer district. Your responsibility usually ends at the connection, so confirm with the town before paying for work outside your property.

How long does a sewer repair take?

A spot repair may take a day. Full replacement or lining takes longer depending on length and access, and we give a schedule before starting.

Do you repair sewer lines in nearby towns?

Yes. The same crews serve Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

.
