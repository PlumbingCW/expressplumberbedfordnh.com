Where Joppa Hill is

.

Joppa Hill Road runs through the western part of Bedford, and Campbell Road is nearby. Pulpit Rock Conservation Area, which is town-owned, lies in the northwestern part of town on New Boston Road.

We do not publish a street-by-street boundary, because none that we could verify exists. If you are on or near these roads, call and the dispatcher will confirm your address is in our work area.

Know your system before you call

.

Some New Hampshire homes use public water and sewer, and others use a private well and a septic system. The answer is not the same from street to street, and we do not assume it for Joppa Hill.

Your water bill tells you whether a utility serves you. The Bedford town offices can tell you about sewer or septic for your address. Telling the dispatcher what you have lets us bring the right parts and plan the visit.

If you have a private well

.

A private well changes what a plumber looks at. Low pressure, sputtering faucets and a pump that cycles often can come from the pump, the pressure tank or the pipes, so we check each one in turn.

The New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services recommends testing a private well every three to five years. We do not test water quality, so use a state-accredited lab and ask us about treatment equipment afterward.

Winter and the thaw

.

New Hampshire winters put pressure on pipes in unheated spaces. Know where your main shutoff is, and keep the path to it clear.

If a pipe has frozen or split, close the main and call. Burst pipe repair covers the first steps and the repair that follows.

Common questions

.
Do Joppa Hill homes use well water?

We do not know which homes do. It varies by address, so check your water bill or ask the town, and tell the dispatcher what you have.

Can you repair a well pump or pressure tank?

Tell the dispatcher what the pump and tank are doing, and we will say whether the visit is something we can handle. Pressure and cycling problems are checked in a set order.

Should I test my well water?

The state recommends testing every three to five years. Use an accredited lab, since we do not test water quality ourselves.

Is Joppa Hill inside your work area?

Our office is in Bedford and we take calls in the town. Give the dispatcher your street so the plumber knows where to go.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. For private wells, the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services recommends testing every three to five years.
