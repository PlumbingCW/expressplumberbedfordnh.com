Bathroom Rough-In Plumbing in a Basement in Bedford, NH

.

Finishing a basement with a bathroom adds real value, and it adds real plumbing questions. Waste has to flow out of a room that may sit below the sewer line, and the rough-in has to be right before the concrete patch goes down.

This guide walks through the stages, the key decision about an ejector pump and the planning that prevents expensive rework.

By the Bedford Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-04

What rough-in means

.

Rough-in is the installation of drains, vents and supply lines before walls and floors are finished. It sets where the toilet, sink and shower connect.

Everything after that, the tile, drywall, fixtures and trim, depends on rough-in being accurate. A drain off by two inches is a problem that cannot be fixed with trim.

Gravity drain or ejector pump

.

The first question is whether the bathroom sits above or below your main sewer line.

Situation.How waste leaves.What it needs.
Bathroom above the main line.Gravity.Properly sloped drain and vent.
Bathroom below the main line.Ejector pump.Sealed pit, pump, check valve and vent.
Only a sink or a laundry tub below the line.Sump-style pump or gravity.Depends on the fixture.

A basement slab is often below the exit point of the main sewer, so many basement baths need an ejector or a sewage pump. Locate the main line and measure its depth before you decide.

Cutting the slab

.

If the drains must run below the floor, a section of slab is cut open, trenches are dug and pipe is laid with proper slope. The slab is then patched with new concrete.

  1. Mark the layout of the toilet, shower and sink on the floor.
  2. Cut and remove the concrete along the drain path.
  3. Dig the trench and set drain and vent lines with slope.
  4. Test the drain with water or air before covering it.
  5. Backfill and pour the patch.
  6. Wait for the concrete to cure before framing and finishing.

Dust and noise are part of the job, and finished areas nearby should be protected.

Venting and supply lines

.

Every drain needs a vent, which lets air in so water can flow and keeps sewer gas out of the room. In a basement, vents often tie into an existing stack or run up through a wall.

Supply lines come from the main hot and cold lines nearby. Shutoff valves at each fixture make later repairs easier, and freezing risk must be considered if lines run along exterior walls.

Permits and inspections

.

A new bathroom is a significant plumbing change, and most towns require permits and inspections. Inspectors usually want to see the rough-in before it is covered.

Ask the Bedford building department what applies, and schedule the inspection before the slab is patched or the walls are closed. See plumbing installation for how we coordinate with contractors.

Water in the basement first

.

A basement with water problems is no place for a new bathroom. Check for dampness, floor drains that back up and a sump that runs a lot.

If the space is wet, basement waterproofing comes first, and a camera check of the main line with a sewer camera inspection shows whether it can carry the added load.

Planning the layout before anyone cuts concrete

.

Draw the room to scale and place the toilet first, since it needs the most clearance. Leave generous clear space beside and in front of the toilet, and confirm the minimum clearances with the inspector before you frame.

Then place the shower or tub, then the vanity. Keeping fixtures close together shortens drain and vent runs, which lowers the cost and the number of places a joint can fail. A bathroom tucked against the existing plumbing wall is usually far cheaper than one across the basement.

Heat, moisture and finishing details

.

A basement bathroom adds humidity to a space that may already be damp. Plan for an exhaust fan that vents outdoors, not into the ceiling, and for a floor finish that tolerates moisture.

Supply lines run through spaces that can be cold, so insulate any pipe near an exterior wall. Leave an access panel behind the shower valve and near the ejector pit, so a future repair does not mean demolishing tile.

Common questions

.
Do I need a permit for a basement bathroom?

Most towns require one. Ask the Bedford building department.

Will I need an ejector pump?

If the bathroom is below the main sewer line, yes. A plumber can measure your line's depth.

How long does the rough-in take?

A bathroom rough-in often takes a few days, plus time for the concrete patch to cure.

Can I rough in before I finish the walls?

Yes, and that is the normal order. The inspector will want to see it before it is covered.

What happens if the floor drain backs up?

A backup suggests a main line problem. Have it cleared and checked before adding a bathroom.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project.
