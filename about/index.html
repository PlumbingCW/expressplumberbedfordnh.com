About Bedford Express Plumbing

.

We are a licensed and insured plumbing company based in Bedford, New Hampshire. We built our service around two ideas: pick up the phone at any hour, and tell people the price before the work starts.

Call (603) 815-9179.

What we do

.

We repair, clean and install plumbing for homes and small businesses in Bedford and the nearby towns. Our work ranges from drain cleaning and water heater repair to sewer line repair and plumbing installation.

We do not do burner or combustion work on heating systems. When a call needs a heating technician, we say so.

How we work

.

The line is answered by a person, 24/7. Same-day appointments are offered when a plumber is free, and emergencies come first.

  • Clear price before work starts.
  • Same-day appointments when available.
  • Licensed and insured.
  • Plain answers, including when a repair is not worth the money.

You can verify any New Hampshire plumber by contacting the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification directly.

How we price work

.

A plumber explains the problem and the price before work starts, and you approve it before anything begins. If the situation changes once a wall is open or a pipe is exposed, we stop and explain the new price before continuing.

We explain what any visit includes and what it costs before you agree to it, so the first number you hear is the one you pay.

Licensing and how to check it

.

New Hampshire plumbers are licensed by the state, and the Office of Professional Licensure and Certification handles it. Plumbers do not appear in that office's online lookup, so contact the office directly if you want to confirm any plumber's license, ours included.

Insurance matters too. Ask any contractor for a certificate of insurance before they start work in your house, and ask the same of us.

The kind of work we turn down

.

We do not do burner, furnace or combustion work. That belongs to a licensed heating technician, and we say so when a call turns out to be one.

We also tell you when a repair is not worth the money. A water heater near the end of its life or a pipe that keeps failing is often better replaced, and we would rather explain that than sell you a fix that will not last. For larger work, see repiping or water heater replacement.

What to expect when a plumber arrives

.

The plumber introduces themselves, asks you to show them the problem and listens to what you have seen before they touch anything. They look at the fixture, the pipe or the equipment, and they explain what they found in plain words.

  1. You hear the cause of the problem and the options for fixing it.
  2. You hear the price for each option before any work starts.
  3. You choose, and the work begins only after you approve it.
  4. The plumber tests the repair and shows you what was done.
  5. You get a written record of the work and any advice for next time.

If the plumber finds something else, such as corroded valves or a failing water heater, they tell you what they saw and leave the decision to you. Nothing is added to the job without your approval.

A note on emergencies

.

A plumbing emergency is any situation where waiting makes damage worse. Water that will not stop, sewage in the house and a tank that has failed all qualify, and our dispatchers are trained to put those calls first.

If you are not sure whether your problem is an emergency, call anyway. A dispatcher can tell you in a minute whether a plumber should come now or whether the next opening is soon enough. See burst pipe repair and sewer line repair for what the most urgent calls involve.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project.
