Plumbing Installation in Bedford, NH

.

Plumbing installation is the planned work of adding or moving fixtures and lines, from a new sink to a full bathroom rough-in. It is booked ahead, and our dispatch line is open 24/7 to schedule a visit.

In Bedford, NH most installation work follows a remodel, an addition or a finished basement, when a new bathroom, laundry or kitchen needs water, drains and vents in places they have never been.

Rough-in and finish: two stages

.

Most projects happen in two visits. The rough-in comes first, when walls and floors are open, and sets pipes, drains and vents in the right places. After the walls are closed and tiled, the finish stage sets fixtures, connects them and tests everything.

We coordinate with your contractor on timing. If you are acting as your own project manager, tell us when framing will be complete so we are there before the drywall.

Basements, additions and finished spaces

.

Basements and additions raise special questions. A basement bathroom below the sewer line needs an ejector pump to lift waste, and the pit and lid have to be sealed and vented. A bump-out addition may need new vent stacks through the roof.

In Bedford, where many homes were built after 1950 with unfinished basements, adding a bath downstairs is common. Slab cutting, pit work and venting need a plan before the demolition, and basement waterproofing is worth settling first if the space has ever been damp.

Codes, permits and inspections

.

Plumbing work follows model codes such as the International Plumbing Code, and the adopted edition and local amendments are set by the town. Ask the Bedford building department which edition applies and whether your project needs a permit.

We work to the code the town enforces and tell you when an inspection is needed so your project is documented. Unpermitted work can cause problems when you sell the house.

Choosing fixtures

.

Fixtures drive both appearance and cost. A few choices matter more than others.

  • Check the rough-in dimensions on a toilet before you buy.
  • Shower valves are installed behind the wall, so choose one with replaceable cartridges.
  • Faucets with widely available parts are easier to repair later.
  • Water-efficient models may also qualify for rebates from some providers.

If you already have a fixture that has failed, see faucet and sink repair or toilet repair and installation.

Common questions

.
Can you work with my contractor?

Yes. We coordinate rough-in and finish visits around the framing, tile and drywall schedule.

Do I need a permit for a new bathroom?

Most towns require one for new plumbing. Ask the Bedford building department for the process.

Can I supply my own fixtures?

Yes. Tell us the model before the visit so we can check rough-in dimensions and parts.

What is an ejector pump?

A pump that lifts waste from a below-grade bathroom up to the sewer line. It is needed when a fixture sits lower than the main drain.

How long does a bathroom rough-in take?

A single bath often takes a day or two, and longer when a slab must be cut.

Do you install in other towns?

Yes. We cover Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. The EPA WaterSense label identifies water-efficient toilets and faucets.
