Installing a New Toilet in Bedford, NH

.

Replacing a toilet is one of the more manageable plumbing projects for a handy homeowner, and one of the easiest to get subtly wrong. A bowl set a little off level or on a worn wax ring can leak for months before anyone sees it.

This guide walks through the measurements, the steps and the checks that matter, and it tells you when a plumber is the better call.

Before you buy: measure the rough-in

.

The rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor drain flange. The most common size is 12 inches, though 10 and 14 inch rough-ins exist, and a toilet made for one will not fit another.

Measure to the center of the bolts that hold the old toilet down, not to the edge of the bowl. Note whether the bowl is round or elongated, and check the space in front of it so a longer bowl will not crowd a door or vanity.

What you need

.

Gather everything before you shut off the water, because the house will be without that toilet for an hour or two.

  • A new toilet, seat and wax ring, or a wax-free seal.
  • New closet bolts and a new supply line.
  • An adjustable wrench, a putty knife and a level.
  • A bucket, towels and a sponge.
  • Gloves and a helper, since a toilet is heavy and awkward.

Removing the old toilet

.

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to close it, flush to empty the tank and sponge out what remains. Disconnect the supply line, remove the nuts from the closet bolts and rock the bowl gently to break the seal.

Lift straight up and set the old toilet on a towel or a sheet of cardboard. Stuff a rag in the open flange to block sewer gas, and scrape off the old wax with a putty knife.

Check the flange and the floor

.

This is the step that decides whether the new toilet will last. The flange should sit flat on top of the finished floor, be free of cracks and have no rot in the wood around it.

If the floor is soft or stained, stop and repair it before you set the bowl. A cracked flange can often be fixed with a repair ring, though a flange that sits below the floor surface or a wet subfloor is a job for a plumber, and sometimes a carpenter.

Setting the new bowl

.

Place the wax ring on the flange or on the bottom of the bowl, then lower the toilet straight down over the closet bolts. Press down firmly with your body weight and do not rock it afterward, because that breaks the seal.

  1. Thread washers and nuts onto the bolts and tighten them alternately, a little at a time.
  2. Stop when the bowl no longer moves, since over-tightening cracks porcelain.
  3. Check level in two directions and shim the low side if needed.
  4. Cut the bolts to length and snap on the caps.
  5. Set the tank on its gasket, tighten the tank bolts evenly and attach the supply line.
  6. Open the shutoff slowly, let the tank fill and flush several times while you watch every joint.

When to call a plumber instead

.

A straight swap is within reach for many people. Some situations are not, and it is worth recognizing them early.

Situation.Why a plumber helps.
Flange broken, below the floor or loose.Needs a proper repair so the seal holds.
Soft or stained subfloor.May indicate a long-term leak and structural damage.
Shutoff valve will not close.Replacing it is part of the job and needs the main off.
New toilet in a basement below the sewer.Needs a pump and proper venting.
Toilet keeps clogging after the swap.The trap, vent or main line may be the cause.

Bedford note: the supply side of your toilet

.

Manchester Water Works, which supplies parts of Bedford along the South River Road area, reported in its 2018 consumer confidence report that its lead 90th percentile was under 1 part per billion.

That figure describes the utility's supply, not the pipes inside your house. Solder in homes built before 1986 may still contain lead, so if you are replacing supply lines or valves in an older house, ask a plumber to check what you have and test your tap if you are unsure.

Common questions

.
Can I install a toilet myself?

Many homeowners can, if the flange and floor are in good shape. If the flange is damaged or the floor is soft, call a plumber before you set the bowl.

How long does a toilet installation take?

A straight swap takes a couple of hours for a plumber and a bit longer for a first-time DIY job.

Should I use a wax ring or a wax-free seal?

Both work when the flange is sound. A wax-free seal is forgiving if you must lift the bowl again, and a wax ring is the traditional choice.

Does Bedford's town water affect my new toilet?

Manchester Water Works reported soft water for 2018, so mineral buildup in the tank is mild on that supply. Homes on private wells should test the water, since iron and minerals can stain a bowl.

How do I tell if the old toilet leaked into the floor?

Look for stains around the base, a soft spot when you press the floor with a screwdriver or a musty smell. Any of those means call a plumber before you install.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. The EPA WaterSense label identifies water-efficient toilets and faucets.
