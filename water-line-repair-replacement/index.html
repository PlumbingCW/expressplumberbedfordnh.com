Emergency Water Line Repair in Bedford, NH

.

A broken water service line can flood a yard, drop your pressure to a trickle or leave the house with no water at all. Our 24/7 line takes the call at any hour, and while you wait you should close the main shutoff inside the house and keep people away from soft or sunken ground.

Water line repair and replacement covers the pipe that brings water from the street main or private well into your home. In Bedford, NH that line can run a long way on larger lots, and a failure is often found first on the water bill.

When a water line break is an emergency

.

A line that has failed between the street and the house leaks until someone shuts it off, and the water has nowhere to go but up through the soil.

  • Water bubbling up in the yard, driveway or along the foundation.
  • Sudden drop in pressure or no water at all.
  • A meter that runs fast after every fixture is off.
  • Muddy or discolored water after a pressure change.

Call and tell the dispatcher you suspect the supply line. Same-day assessment is offered when a plumber is free, and the line is answered at night and on weekends.

  1. Close the main shutoff inside the house.
  2. If water is still flowing, find the curb stop or meter valve and call your water provider.
  3. Mark the wet spot with a stake or flag and keep children and pets away.
  4. Photograph the area before it changes.

Public water or a private well

.

Your supply source changes who owns what. If you are on a public system, such as Manchester Water Works, the utility owns the main and usually a short stub. You own the line from the meter or curb stop to the house. Manchester Water Works draws from Lake Massabesic and serves portions of surrounding communities.

If you have a private well, the line from the well to the pressure tank and house is entirely yours. Well owners should also test the water. NHDES recommends testing every three to five years, and radon and arsenic are among the most common contaminants.

Ask your utility where its responsibility ends, and do not dig until utilities are marked.

Signs the line is failing

.

Many water line failures begin slowly. Look for a pattern before the line breaks outright.

Sign.What it can mean.
Water bill climbs with no change in use.A slow leak on the supply side.
Wet, spongy or very green patch of lawn.Water escaping from a buried pipe.
Pressure that drops over weeks.Corrosion, scale or a growing leak.
Rust-colored or cloudy water.Corroded steel or iron service line.
Sound of water when all fixtures are off.Leak between meter and house.

Repair or replace the line

.

A single break in a line that is otherwise sound can be repaired with a short section. A line that is old galvanized steel, leaking in several places or lead is better replaced end to end.

Replacement methods include open trench and trenchless pulling, where a new line is drawn through the path of the old one with limited digging. Trenchless saves a driveway or a landscaped yard, though it needs suitable soil and access points. New lines in modern homes are commonly copper or a type of polyethylene approved for water service.

Frost depth matters in New Hampshire, because a line laid too shallow freezes. We set the new pipe below frost and tell you the depth before digging, and a camera or locator maps the old route first. When water in the yard might be a drain or irrigation leak, a leak detection visit confirms the source.

What affects the cost of water line work

.

These factors drive the price.

Length and depth.A 40-foot line is a very different job from a 200-foot line.
Method.Open trench and trenchless each have their own equipment and restoration.
What is above the line.Driveways, walls, trees and landscaping add restoration.
Pipe material.Copper and polyethylene differ in cost and in how they are joined.
Permits and utility coordination.Work at the meter or street connection may involve the water provider or the town.

Insurance often covers damage caused by a sudden break, while the cost of the line itself is frequently excluded unless you hold service line coverage. Check your policy and any utility-offered protection program.

Common questions

.
Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Bedford?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, and a failed supply line is placed ahead of routine calls. Same-day service is offered when a plumber is free.

How do I know if the leak is between the meter and the house?

Close the main shutoff in the house. If the meter keeps moving, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the shutoff.

Who pays for a water line break in my yard?

The homeowner usually owns the line from the meter or curb stop to the house. Confirm with your utility, and check whether your insurance or a protection program applies.

Can you replace the line without digging up the yard?

Often, with trenchless methods, if the soil and access allow. We assess the route and explain options before work.

Should I test my well water after a repair?

It is a good moment to. NHDES recommends testing private wells every three to five years, and a lab test shows what a filter or treatment may need to address.

Do you repair water lines in nearby towns?

Yes. We cover Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. For private wells, the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services recommends testing every three to five years.
