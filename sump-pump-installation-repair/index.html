When a sump pump failure is an emergency

.

Sump pumps work silently until they do not. When one fails, groundwater rises through the floor drain or slab joint.

  • Water is rising in the pit or on the basement floor.
  • The pump runs constantly without lowering the water.
  • The pump hums or clicks but does not move water.
  • The power is out in a storm and the pump has no backup.

Call at any hour and tell the dispatcher how much water you have. We offer same-day service when a plumber is free, and nights and weekends are covered.

  1. Make sure the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  2. Check that the discharge pipe outside is not blocked or frozen.
  3. Do not touch the pump or wiring while standing in water.
  4. Move valuables off the floor and call us.

Why sump pumps fail

.

Pumps wear out with use, and heavy use accelerates the process. These are the most common failure points.

Failure.Cause.Fix.
Pump will not start.Stuck float switch or dead motor.Free or replace the switch, or replace the pump.
Runs but moves little water.Clogged impeller or failed check valve.Clean the pump, replace valve.
Runs constantly.Stuck float, undersized pump or very high water table.Adjust or upsize the pump.
Loud or rattling.Worn bearings or debris.Replace the pump.
Water outside returns to the pit.Discharge too close to the house.Extend and slope the discharge line.
Dead in an outage.No battery or water-powered backup.Add a backup system.

Why basements in Bedford get wet

.

Bedford borders the Merrimack River, and in spring the rain and snowmelt can saturate the ground around a foundation for weeks. Many of the town's homes are basement houses built after 1950, and a basement slab in wet soil is where water finds its way.

A pit and pump handle groundwater that rises beneath or beside the slab. Roof runoff, a clogged gutter or a downspout that empties at the foundation can add to the load, so we also look at where water is going outside. For long-term water problems, see basement waterproofing.

Installing a new sump pump system

.

A new system starts with the pit. If there is an existing sump, we size the pump to the inflow. If there is not, we cut the slab, dig the pit, set a basin with a lid and run a discharge line to a spot that drains away from the foundation.

A check valve keeps the water from flowing back into the pit, and a battery backup or water-powered backup keeps the pump working during an outage. Many floods happen during storms, and storms are when power fails. Test the pump each spring by pouring a bucket into the pit and watching it start.

Common questions

.
Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Bedford?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and a flooding basement is placed first. Same-day service is offered when a plumber is free.

How long does a sump pump last?

Many last 7 to 10 years, less with heavy use. A pump that is old and runs often is worth replacing before the next storm.

Do I need a battery backup?

If your basement has ever flooded or if outages are common where you live, a backup is worth having. Storms and power failures often arrive together.

Where should the discharge line empty?

Away from the foundation and downhill, on a slope that does not send water back toward the house. In winter the end must not freeze shut.

Can I test my pump myself?

Yes. Pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start, empty it and shut off. If not, call us.
