Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Bedford, NH

.

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons in an hour, so speed matters more than anything else. Close the main water shutoff first, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines, turn off power to any wet area and then call our 24/7 line.

Burst pipe repair is the stopping of the flood, the removal of the failed section and the installation of new pipe, followed by a check for other damage. In Bedford, NH, where winter cold can freeze an exposed line overnight, these calls cluster in the coldest weeks.

Burst pipe emergency: what to do in the first ten minutes

.

The order you act in changes the outcome. Do these steps while you are calling for help.

  1. Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter, where the line enters the house or on the basement wall.
  2. Open the lowest faucet or hose bib to drain remaining water from the pipes.
  3. If water is near outlets or the panel, turn off electricity at the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  4. Move valuables, rugs and electronics out of the wet area.
  5. Take photos and video before you clean, for your insurer.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from.

We take these calls at any hour and place them first. Same-day repair is offered whenever a plumber is free, and nights and weekends are covered.

Frozen pipes and what to do before they burst

.

A pipe usually does not burst where it freezes. It bursts a short distance away, where trapped water pressure builds up between the ice plug and a closed faucet.

If you find a frozen pipe with no leak yet, open the nearest faucet, apply gentle warmth with a hair dryer or heating pad and never use an open flame. If you cannot find or reach the frozen section, or if the pipe is already split, call us.

Why pipes burst in older and newer homes

.

Freezing is the best-known cause, but not the only one. Corroded galvanized steel, aging copper with pinhole leaks and high water pressure all end in a split pipe. A pressure reducing valve that has failed can send street pressure through fixtures meant for much less.

Many Bedford homes have a mix of copper and PEX from past renovations. Joints between the two materials and old fittings behind finished walls are common failure points. If a repair reveals widespread corrosion, repiping is worth discussing.

How the repair works

.

Once the water is off, we find the split, cut out the failed section and join new pipe using fittings that suit the material. Copper is sweated or press-fitted, and PEX uses crimp or expansion connections.

We then restore pressure slowly, check every joint for leaks and open a faucet to purge air. If the flood reached walls or flooring, we point out areas to dry out, and we can help you document the damage for your claim.

When the break is hidden behind a wall or under a floor, leak detection finds the exact spot first, so we cut a small access hole instead of a large one.

Common questions

.
Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Bedford?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, and an active flood is the first call we place. Same-day repair is offered when a plumber is free.

Where is my main water shutoff?

Look near the water meter, where the supply line enters the house, or on a basement or utility room wall. Learn its location now so you can act in seconds.

Will insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe, though not always the pipe itself or damage from neglect. Call your insurer promptly and document everything.

Can a frozen pipe be thawed without a plumber?

Sometimes, with gentle heat and an open faucet. If the pipe has already split or you cannot reach the frozen section, call us.

How quickly can the water be stopped?

If you close the main shutoff, the flood stops immediately. If the shutoff is stuck or missing, tell the dispatcher so we arrive prepared.

Do you repair burst pipes in nearby towns?

Yes. The same crews serve Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

.
