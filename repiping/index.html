Repiping in Bedford, NH

.

Repiping is the replacement of a home's supply piping when repairs stop making sense. If a pipe is actively leaking, call the 24/7 line and use burst pipe repair first, and use repiping when the pattern shows the whole system is failing.

In Bedford, NH most homes were built after 1950, and many still carry galvanized steel or early copper that is now past its working life. A repipe is a planned project that replaces the lines with new PEX or copper and ends the cycle of repeated leaks.

Signs a house needs repiping

.

One leak does not mean a repipe. A pattern does.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different spots, or a second repair within a year.
  • Rusty, brown or metallic-tasting water when you first turn a tap on.
  • Low pressure throughout the house that gets worse over time.
  • Visible corrosion, green stains on copper or flaking on steel pipe.
  • A house built when galvanized pipe was standard that has never been repiped.

If a pipe has burst and water is flowing now, see burst pipe repair and call at any hour. Repiping is for after the emergency, when you decide how to avoid the next one.

Pipe materials compared

.

The three common choices differ in cost, life and install method.

Material.Strengths.Trade-offs.
PEX.Flexible, resists freeze splitting better than rigid pipe, fast to install in finished walls.Not for outdoor sun exposure, needs correct fittings.
Copper.Long track record, durable, recyclable.Higher material cost, can pit in aggressive water.
CPVC.Lower cost, corrosion resistant.More brittle, less common in new work.
Galvanized steel.Not recommended for new work.Corrodes from the inside and restricts flow.

We recommend what fits your house and water. A private well with acidic water may call for different choices than a public supply with soft water.

Water quality and pipe life

.

The water in the pipe affects how long the pipe lasts. Manchester Water Works reported a pH of 7.8 and soft water at 19.35 mg/L total hardness for 2018, which is gentle on piping. Private well water can be acidic or high in iron and is worth testing.

NHDES recommends that private well owners test every three to five years. If your water is corroding pipe, a treatment system or a change in materials may be part of the plan. Lead solder was banned in 1986, so a house older than that may have solder joints worth checking, and a lab test can confirm lead in your water.

How a repipe works and how long it takes

.

We plan the route before we open a wall. Most houses are repiped from the basement up, using the existing chases and closets, and access holes are kept small and patched.

  1. Walk the house and map every fixture, valve and the water heater.
  2. Shut off water, drain the system and set up protection for floors.
  3. Install new mains and branch lines to every fixture.
  4. Connect fixtures, test pressure and check every joint.
  5. Patch access holes, though painting is left to you or a painter.

A typical house takes a few days. Water is usually off for part of each day, and we tell you the schedule so you can plan around it.

Permits and inspections

.

A repipe is a larger plumbing project, and many towns require a permit and an inspection. The adopted plumbing code and the permit process are set by the town, so ask the Bedford building department what applies.

We handle the work to the code the town enforces and can describe what an inspector will want to see.

Common questions

.
How do I know if I need a full repipe or just a repair?

If leaks keep returning in different places, water is discolored or pressure is poor throughout, a repipe is worth pricing. A single leak in a sound system is a repair.

Will repiping make a mess?

We protect floors, keep access holes small and clean daily. Some drywall patching is unavoidable, and we explain it up front.

How long is the water off?

Usually part of each working day. We give the schedule before we start.

PEX or copper, which is better?

Both work. PEX is flexible and quick to install, and copper has a longer record. We recommend based on your house and water.

Do I need a permit?

Probably. Ask the Bedford building department, and we will work to what the town enforces.

Do you repipe homes in nearby towns?

Yes. We cover Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett.

Standards and references

.

Plumbing work follows published model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) from the International Code Council and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The edition a town enforces is its own decision, so ask the Bedford building department which one applies to your project. For private wells, the New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services recommends testing every three to five years.
