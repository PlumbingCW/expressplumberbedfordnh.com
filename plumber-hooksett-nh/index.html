Emergency calls from Hooksett

.

Hooksett combines busy roadways, shops and homes, and the emergencies reflect that.

  • A main line backing up into a basement or lower level.
  • A drain blockage in a shop, restaurant or office during business hours.
  • A burst or frozen pipe in an older home.
  • A water heater leaking onto a finished floor.
  • A water service line that has broken near the street.

Give the dispatcher your street and which side of town you are on. Same-day appointments are offered when a plumber is free, and nights and weekends are handled like any other hour.

Two water precincts and what they mean

.

Hooksett has two village water precincts, the Central Hooksett Water Precinct and the Hooksett Village Water Precinct. Which one serves you, if either, depends on your street, and homes outside them rely on wells.

Ask the precinct who maintains the line to your meter. The part from the meter to the house is the homeowner's, and our water line repair work picks up there. Wells should be tested every three to five years according to NHDES.

Merrimack County, not Hillsborough

.

Hooksett is in Merrimack County, while Bedford is in Hillsborough County, and the town line affects who you call for permits and records. Ask the Hooksett town office what applies to your address, not the Bedford one.
