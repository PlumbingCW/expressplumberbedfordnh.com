What each town page covers

.

Every town has its own page, because the plumbing problems and the water supply differ from place to place. The pages list ZIP codes, the emergencies we see most and what to do while a plumber travels to you.

Manchester is the largest city we serve, with mill-era buildings, multi-unit houses and the Manchester Water Works supply from Lake Massabesic. Merrimack is where private wells and PFAS testing are a real concern. Goffstown sits along the Piscataquog River, where wet basements are common.

Amherst has long driveways and long water lines, and part of the town is served by the Amherst Village District. Hooksett lies in Merrimack County and has two village water precincts.

What to have ready when you call

.

A dispatcher can send the right plumber faster when you have a few details ready. None of them is required, and the dispatcher will ask for what is missing.

  • Your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • What is happening, in plain words, such as a leak, a backup or no hot water.
  • Whether water or sewage is spreading, and whether you can reach the main shutoff.
  • Whether you are on public water or a private well.
  • A phone number where you can be reached on the way.

If you are not sure which service you need, the plumbing services page lists everything we do.

Public water, private wells and what it means for service

.

Across our service area, homes get water in different ways. Manchester Water Works draws from Lake Massabesic and serves Manchester and portions of six surrounding communities. Village districts and precincts serve parts of Goffstown, Merrimack, Hooksett and Amherst, and many homes in every one of these towns use private wells.

The difference matters on a service call. A home on public water has a meter and a utility that owns the main in the street, and a home on a well has a pump, a pressure tank and a line that is entirely the owner's. Tell the dispatcher which you have, and we will bring the right equipment.

New Hampshire's environmental agency recommends that private well owners test their water every three to five years, and some wells in this area have had PFAS concerns. A test result is the right basis for choosing any filter or treatment system.

Getting to you from Bedford

Our crews start from Bedford and reach the surrounding towns mostly by the Everett Turnpike, Route 101 and US 3. Traffic, weather and the number of calls in progress all affect how quickly a plumber arrives, and the dispatcher gives you an honest estimate when you call.

If we are not the best choice for your address, we say so on the phone. A dispatcher who tells you the truth in the first minute saves you an hour of waiting.
