Emergency Hydro Jetting in Bedford, NH

.

A drain that keeps backing up after cable cleaning is a good candidate for jetting, and a line that is fully blocked is an emergency. Our 24/7 line answers at any hour, and while you wait you should stop using water in the house and keep floor drains clear of stored items.

Hydro jetting is the use of a high-pressure water hose with a rotating nozzle to scour grease, scale, sludge and fine roots from the inside of a drain or sewer pipe. In Bedford, NH it is the method we choose when a cable machine only punches a hole through the problem.

When a blocked line is an emergency

.

A slow drain can wait for a scheduled visit. A line that has stopped completely and is sending water back up cannot.

  • Water backs up into a tub, shower or basement floor drain.
  • Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at the same time.
  • A previous cable cleaning gave only a few days of relief.
  • A restaurant or shop kitchen line is blocked during service hours.

Tell the dispatcher what you see in your first sentence. Same-day appointments are offered when a plumber with jetting equipment is free, and calls are answered at night and on weekends.

  1. Stop using water in the house until the line is cleared.
  2. Avoid chemical openers, which can pool in a blocked pipe.
  3. Lift boxes and valuables off the floor near the lowest drain.
  4. Note when the problem began and which fixtures are affected.

How hydro jetting works

.

A jetter pumps water at high pressure through a flexible hose to a nozzle with forward and rear-facing jets. The forward jet breaks the blockage, and the rear jets pull the hose through the pipe while they scrub the wall in all directions.

Because the water does the cleaning, the pipe wall is left smooth instead of just opened up. That is why jetting tends to last longer on grease and scale than a cable cutter that leaves a film behind.

We run a camera first when the line has a history of trouble. The picture tells us whether the pipe can take the pressure and which nozzle to use, and the same camera confirms the result afterward.

.

Both tools have a place, and the right choice depends on what is in the pipe.

Kitchen lines, restaurants and older homes

.

Grease is the clearest reason to jet. It cools in the pipe, hardens into a ring that narrows the bore and catches food, and a cable simply drills through the middle of it. Kitchens in shops, restaurants and multi-unit buildings in Bedford's office parks see this often, and a regular jetting schedule keeps lines open between busy periods.

Houses built before 1986 may still have lead-soldered joints in water supply lines, which is a separate matter from drain piping, but older cast iron and clay drains carry scale that jetting handles well. If a camera shows a cracked or collapsed section, jetting is the wrong tool and we move to sewer line repair instead.

Roots and the spring thaw

.

Tree roots enter a drain line at joints, and fine roots grow into a mat that catches paper and waste. Cables cut the thick strands, but a jet with a root-cutting nozzle clears the mat and rinses it downstream.

Spring thaw brings groundwater and shifting soil to New Hampshire yards, and lines that already leaked at a joint can start to clog just as the ground wakes up. If your drains slow after the snow melts, a camera inspection before jetting is worth the extra step.

Common questions

.
Do you handle hydro jetting emergencies at night or on weekends in Bedford?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, and a fully blocked line is placed ahead of routine calls. Same-day jetting is offered when a plumber with the equipment is free.

Will hydro jetting damage my pipes?

On sound pipe, no. We run a camera first on older or suspect lines, and we pick the pressure and nozzle for the pipe material. Cracked or collapsed pipe is not jetted.

How often should a kitchen line be jetted?

It depends on volume. A home kitchen may never need it, while a busy commercial kitchen is often jetted on a regular schedule set by how fast grease builds.

Can jetting remove tree roots permanently?

No. It clears the roots that are in the pipe, but they will regrow if the joint is open. A repair or lining closes the entry point.

Is jetting loud or messy?

The machine is loud, and a small amount of water may come out of the cleanout. We lay down protection and set up where the mess stays contained.

Do you jet lines in other towns?

Yes. We serve Manchester, Merrimack, Goffstown, Amherst and Hooksett from the same line.
